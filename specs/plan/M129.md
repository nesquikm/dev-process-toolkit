# Implementation Plan

## M129 — Deliver Pipeline Contract Repair and Resume {#M129}

**Goal:** Repair the `/deliver` orchestrator contract against two 2026-08-17 field reports from a live three-repo run, and give the pipeline the two entry shapes it lacks — a milestone that names its own repo, and a milestone resumed mid-pipeline.

**Prerequisites:** M128 merged (v2.67.1). No hard coupling; the ordering is the program's serial invariant.

**Release target:** v2.68.0 "Compact" (provisional; minor — five `Added` FRs alongside two `Fixed`)

**Migration:** `none` — every change is additive with unchanged defaults. The optional plan-level target-repo declaration, the gate taxonomy, and the conversational merge-policy override all leave existing consumer artifacts behaving byte-identically when they say nothing.

### Risks

| Category | Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unclear acceptance criteria | **AC-STE-492.4 has a chicken-and-egg problem.** It requires the fixture to assert against a captured worker stage report, but no such capture can exist until the fence itself ships — so the first implementation has nothing real to point at, and the easy escape is a fixture that quietly asserts against hand-written text, which is the exact vacuity this FR exists to kill. | high | Commit a hand-authored capture fixture that is explicitly labelled as a model of the emission, and make AC-STE-492.5's mutation pair the thing that gives it teeth. | Breaking changes | Two FRs each register a gate-check probe (AC-STE-493.6, AC-STE-494.5). A single probe registration has historically flipped pinned counts across seven test files. | medium | Land both probe registrations in one sweep rather than two, so the count-pin update happens once. |
| Unclear acceptance criteria | STE-493 and STE-494 each sit against a shipped AC that reads as forbidding them. A reviewer who does not follow the argument will correctly reject the change. | medium | AC-STE-493.8 and AC-STE-494.7 require the supersession argument in writing inside the FR, not in the commit message or a review thread. |
| Performance impact | Milestone size — seven FRs, 49 ACs, four skills — raises the chance of a defect in the specs themselves rather than the code. No high-severity risk is left unmitigated; the AC-STE-492.4 risk is accepted with the mitigation above rather than resolved, because it cannot be resolved before the fence exists.

### Notes

- **Scope was raised three times and enlarged three times.** The drafting session recommended a smaller milestone at each of three decision points — deferring multi-repo routing, keeping draft-PR support out, and splitting the resume work into its own milestone. The operator chose the larger scope each time. Seven FRs and 49 ACs across four skills is at the top of this repo's historical range while also being unusually cross-cutting; the risk is recorded here rather than smoothed over.
- **The fence FR is the one that is broken as shipped.** STE-492's contract, followed literally, would have halted three fully successful milestone chains. The other six are gaps rather than active breakage.
- **Two supersessions must be argued, not assumed.** STE-493 and STE-494 each sit against a shipped AC that reads as forbidding them (STE-313 AC.5 and STE-463 AC.3 respectively). Both are compatible for reasons that are real but non-obvious — disjoint tty/headless domains, and a ratchet whose transitions cannot produce `auto`. Each FR carries an AC requiring the argument in writing, because a reviewer who does not see it will correctly kill the change.
- **Verification is deliberately two-tier.** Deterministic modules plus probes plus mutation-verified guards for exactly the two rules whose violation is unrecoverable — the irreversible-gate-class exclusion and the merge-policy ratchet. Everything else is prose with targeted literal probes. The tier boundary is "would a silent drift here cause an action that cannot be undone", and it should be defended on that basis in review rather than relitigated per-FR.
- **STE-492 must not be verified the way its own defect was introduced.** All eighteen shipped STE-464 ACs are greps of `/deliver`'s own SKILL prose, which is precisely why a fence with no producer passed every one of them. AC-STE-492.4 and .5 require a real worker capture as the subject.
- **STE-498 is assembly, not new machinery — verified before drafting.** All four cited helpers (`plan_task_state`, `active_plan_ship_ready`, `plan_ship_coherence`, `needs_technical_review_consistency`) and all three cited probes (#40, #63, #75) exist on the current tree, and probe #75 already ships a runnable CLI entry.
- **The multi-repo fix is cheaper than reported.** `/spec-write` binds tracker and specs to its own working directory, so moving the call into the target repo's worker rebinds naturally. No alternate-root plumbing is needed; the source report's cost estimate is higher than the actual one.
