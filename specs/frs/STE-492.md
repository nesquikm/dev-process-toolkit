# Ceremony stages must emit the hand-off fence they are graded on

## Summary

The deliver orchestrator requires every ceremony stage to end its report with a fenced hand-off block, gives one scoped retry when the shape is wrong, and then halts the milestone by name. No ceremony skill emits one, and none of the three even mentions the orchestrator. The block's name appears only on the consuming side and in the consumer's own tests, so there is no producer anywhere in the toolkit.

A live three-repo run produced three fully successful chains that this contract, followed literally, would have halted three times over. The extended reference says the worker kickoff text carries the contract; the operative skill file says the kickoff carries only the effort keyword, and the reference is explicitly not required reading. Every existing check for this contract greps the orchestrator's own prose, which is why the gap shipped undetected.

## Requirement

A stage's hand-off contract reaches the stage that must satisfy it, through the only channel a spawned worker actually reads, and the fence requirement is verified against what a worker really emits rather than against the orchestrator's own prose.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-STE-492.1: The kickoff-task-text step in `skills/deliver/SKILL.md` names the full `deliver-stage-result` contract — banner, the six sections in fixed order, the line cap, and the `- (none found)` fallback — on the operative surface, not only in `docs/deliver-reference.md`.
- AC-STE-492.2: `skills/deliver/SKILL.md` and `docs/deliver-reference.md` agree on what the kickoff text carries; the present disagreement between them is gone.
- AC-STE-492.3: The contract states which sections a stage in a repo without toolkit ceremony omits, so a reduced chain cannot violate a contract written for the full one.
- AC-STE-492.4: A smoke fixture group asserts the fence against a captured worker stage report — an artifact a worker actually produced — and fails when that capture carries no fence.
- AC-STE-492.5: The AC.4 clause is mutation-verified: it fails on a capture with the fence removed and on a capture whose sections are reordered, and it cannot pass by matching `/deliver`'s own SKILL text.
- AC-STE-492.6: The existing assertion that only greps `skills/deliver/SKILL.md` for the literal `deliver-stage-result` no longer stands as the sole evidence for this contract.

## Technical Design

The token appears in exactly five places, all on the consumer side: `skills/deliver/SKILL.md`, `docs/deliver-reference.md`, `tests/m123-ste-464-deliver-skill.test.ts`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and the archived STE-464. It appears zero times in `skills/implement/SKILL.md`, `skills/ship-milestone/SKILL.md`, and `skills/pr/SKILL.md`, none of which mentions `/deliver` at all.

Two candidate fixes were weighed. Teaching the three ceremony skills to emit the fence would make them emit a `/deliver`-specific block during solo runs, where no orchestrator is listening, and would ripple through their probe pins. Making the kickoff text the channel is the smaller change and is what `docs/deliver-reference.md` line 49 already half-states — the worker synthesizes the fence after each in-session stage because the kickoff told it to. The defect is that the operative surface (`skills/deliver/SKILL.md` step 2) names only the effort keyword, and the reference is marked "not required reading" — so on a real run the contract never reaches the worker.

Verification follows the producer/consumer asymmetry rule from STE-485 and STE-396: assert what the runtime actually emits, verified against a real capture, never against prose. All eighteen shipped STE-464 ACs are prose-greps of `/deliver`'s own SKILL, which is exactly why a fence with no producer passed every one of them.

## Testing

Smoke fixture group asserting the fence against a captured worker stage report, with the capture as the subject rather than the SKILL text. Mutation coverage per AC.5: fence removed from the capture and sections reordered in the capture must both go RED, and the clause must not be satisfiable by `/deliver`'s own prose. Cross-surface agreement between SKILL and reference asserted directly. `bun test` full root from `plugins/dev-process-toolkit`.

## Notes

Origin: 2026-08-17 field report from a live three-repo `/deliver` run (dpt 2.67.1) delivering across two Jira-bound repos and one repo with no toolkit. Finding 4 of five.

The reporter's framing — "the two skills' hand-off contracts simply do not compose" — is slightly stronger than the evidence. The reference does state that kickoff carries the fence contract; the failure is that the operative surface drops it and the reference is optional reading. The fix is therefore promotion plus a real producer channel, not a redesign of the hand-off.

This is the third recorded instance of the producer/consumer asymmetry class in this repo (M116 logged it as "producer/consumer asymmetry x3"). The recurrence is part of the record.
