# The two M143 scanners grade the tree, not just their own suites

## Summary

M143 shipped two scanners over the driven path and registered neither.

Both are read only by their own suites, so a clause dropped from a shipped surface reds one test file and never the gate.

The reason they were not registered is the coupling the sibling FR removes: a registration costs a line the reachability pin counts.

With that cost gone, registering them is a change, not a milestone.

## Requirement

Both driven-path scanners are numbered `/gate-check` probes, so the gate reads the shipped tree they were written to protect. Registering them does not make the reachability pin rise, and it moves the pin at most once, through the one place the sibling FR establishes.

## Technical Design

Each scanner gains a probe wrapper that renders its violations in the canonical shape and nothing else; the scanning logic is untouched, so what the suites already prove about it still holds.

Each module gains a command-line entry point, which is both the front door the reachability probe requires of an ordered module and the thing that makes the registration an order a reader can obey.

The registry gains two numbered entries, and the count they carry is propagated through the enumerated pin table the previous cascade established, re-derived from the shipped registry rather than typed.

## Testing

The probe wrappers are driven over fixture trees built on disk, mutated one clause at a time, so a leg that reds names the clause it lost.

The front doors are executed as subprocesses and their exit status read, because a guard that exists and does nothing is the failure this FR exists to remove.

## Notes

Two probes rather than one. A single registration naming both modules would grade two unrelated subjects under one number, and a reader following a red would not know which surface to open.
