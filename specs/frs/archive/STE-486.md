# Phase 4's claim witness must inherit fixture 10a's sampling-gap carve-out

## Summary

A recent tightening requires the run's evidence log to name the commit that claimed the work. The sampler that writes that log fires once per polling call, so a run that claims and finishes inside a single call records only the state from before the claim — and is failed for it, despite the claim being right there in history and the lock correctly released. The sibling fixture that reads the same log already anticipates exactly this and refuses to fail on it. Two halves of one mechanism disagree about identical evidence.

## Requirement

The Phase 4 release-proof row inherits the carve-out its sibling already defines: a missing claim line, when the claim commit is independently confirmed, is reported as a sampling gap and does not fail. Fixture 10a's own carve-out is not tightened — that direction is out of scope by construction (STE-456 AC.2), because a group that reddens on a healthy run gets disabled, which is worse than the hole.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-STE-486.1: The Phase 4 row reports a sampling gap and does not fail when the claim line is absent but the claim commit is independently confirmed in history and the lock is absent.
- AC-STE-486.2: The row still fails when the claim commit genuinely does not exist — the carve-out narrows the failure to the real defect rather than removing it.
- AC-STE-486.3: Fixture 10a's three-case outcome rule and its `10a-sampling-gap` carve-out are byte-unchanged.
- AC-STE-486.4: The carve-out's wording is shared between the two sites rather than restated, so they cannot drift apart again.
- AC-STE-486.5: STE-483's harness proves the row still fails on a capture where the claim genuinely never happened.

## Technical Design

Measured on the none leg: the line read `claim-commit none` while the claim commit existed at `b17c93f` and the lock was correctly absent. The poll call began around 16:06:41 and `/implement` claimed at 16:07:19 — 38 seconds later — then completed inside that same call, so the sampler never fired again and both its `lock-*` and `claim-commit` lines recorded pre-claim state.

STE-451's fixture 10a defines exactly this case and resolves it the other way: an empty sample log with 10b's claim commit present means the lock demonstrably existed and the sampler's cadence missed it, so the group renders PASS and the gap surfaces as its own finding. STE-456 then tightened the Phase 4 row consuming the same log without giving it the equivalent carve-out. The fix is to make the Phase 4 row consult the same authority 10a consults, not to loosen the failure condition generally.

AC-STE-486.4 exists because this defect is a drift between two statements of one rule. Sharing the wording is what prevents the third divergence.

## Testing

Row evaluated against three captures: sampling gap with claim commit present (expect report, not fail), claim genuinely absent (expect fail), and claim line present naming a real sha (expect pass). Byte-comparison test pinning 10a's carve-out unchanged. `bun test` full root.
