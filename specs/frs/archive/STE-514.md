# The confirm gate shows the command's bytes, not a retelling

## Summary

Six of the orchestrator's decisions are ordered from code today, and the operator sees whatever the reader chose to write down. On a real run the reader typed its own one-line rendering of the resume state and approved it, and nothing could tell that apart from a real classification, because nothing was comparing the two.

The gate now shows exactly what the command printed. Not a summary of it, not a table built from it — its bytes. A retelling is a contract violation and is caught by comparing the two, not by asking anyone to be careful.

Placement stops being something anyone can argue with. The operator may reorder or drop steps at the gate; they may not move a step between running here and running in a spawned worker. That removes the undefined phrase the failed run leaned on, by deleting it rather than defining it.

## Requirement

The confirm gate renders the decision command's output verbatim, and no edit at that gate can change where a step runs.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-STE-514.1: The orchestrator's operative surface carries one runnable command line that produces the decision record, written so it can be copied and executed.
- AC-STE-514.2: The six prose orders naming the delegated modules are gone from that surface, replaced by the one command line. No retired order survives as a second instruction.
- AC-STE-514.3: A predicate compares what the gate rendered against the command's captured output and returns false when the rendering is a retelling rather than the bytes.
- AC-STE-514.4: AC.3 is falsified against the real failure: the one-line prose rendering a worker actually produced and self-approved is a fixture, and the predicate returns false on it. A predicate that accepts it fails the test.
- AC-STE-514.5: The predicate returns false for an absent gate and false for a paraphrased gate, and those two are distinguishable in its reasons — a paraphrase is not reported as an absence.
- AC-STE-514.6: The operative surface states that an edit at the gate may reorder or drop steps and may never change a step's placement.
- AC-STE-514.7: The undefined phrase naming the invoking session is deleted from the surface, not redefined. Zero occurrences remain.
- AC-STE-514.8: The operative surface states the operational rule that the delivery pipeline is the top of a pipeline and never a step inside one, naming what to key into a spawned worker instead.
- AC-STE-514.9: The spawn pre-flight probe gains a trigger on the resume path, which today never enters the phase the probe is mandated before.
- AC-STE-514.10: Sibling-surface parity: every clause AC.1, AC.6 and AC.8 place on the operative surface is asserted present on that surface AND asserted consistent with the companion reference document. A rule landing on one and not the other fails.
- AC-STE-514.11: AC.10 is mutation-verified in both directions — deleting the clause from either surface alone turns the assertion red.

## Technical Design

The verbatim requirement needs a predicate, not a rule. `verifyResumeGateRender(rendered, capturedStdout)` answers one question: does the rendered gate contain the command's output as a contiguous run of bytes? Containment rather than equality, because the gate legitimately wraps the record in its own prompt text.

AC.4 is the criterion that decides whether this FR is worth anything. The real run rendered the resume state as a short prose line of its own composition. An acceptance criterion asserting only that a gate was shown would pass on that run, which is why the fixture is the actual emission and the assertion is that the predicate rejects it.

AC.5 separates two failures that a single boolean would blur. An absent gate and a paraphrased gate need different remedies, and reporting a paraphrase as an absence sends the reader looking for the wrong thing.

Deleting the undefined phrase rather than defining it is the design's decision and it is deliberate. Defining it invites a reading where being spawned licenses running inline. Making placement unconditional means the question is never asked.

AC.10 and AC.11 address the drift class this repository hit three times in one milestone and again in the next, where a rule lands on the operative surface and not on its neighbour, or the two disagree in a detail. The parity assertion reads both surfaces and compares; the mutation test deletes from each in turn.

## Testing

The predicate gets a fixture set: a faithful render that must pass, the real paraphrase that must fail, an absent gate that must fail with a distinguishable reason, and a render that contains the bytes inside surrounding prompt text and must pass.

Surface assertions read the shipped files rather than restating their content, so a clause reworded but kept still passes and a clause deleted fails. Parity is asserted by comparing the two surfaces, and mutation-verified by deleting from each alone.

## Notes

The retelling criterion is stated before the surface criterion deliberately: the surface change is only worth making because something can now tell a retelling from the real thing.
