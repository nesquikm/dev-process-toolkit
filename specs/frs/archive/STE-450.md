# Add fixture group 9 — mode:none identity surfaces

## Summary

The runtime fixture roster has one group that runs on a single leg, and it exists because the probe it tests is meaningless on the other. That exemption is the only evidence today that the roster's leg field is genuinely a list rather than a two-state flag wearing a list's clothing.

This adds the mirror on the other side: a group that runs only on the tracker-less leg. Its existence proves the field is real N-way data, and it covers identity surfaces no tracker leg can reach.

Under tracker-less mode two gate probes switch into their enforcing arms rather than going vacuous. One requires a locally minted identity and forbids a tracker block; the other requires a minted milestone whose recorded identity derives its own filename. Neither arm is exercised anywhere today.

The group also carries a cheap cross-mode leak check. A probe that goes vacuous under tracker-less mode should say so through the shared skip-reason renderer, and its skip text should not tell a tracker-less project it needs a tracker's MCP server.

## Requirement

Add fixture group 9 to the canonical roster, scoped to the `none` leg only, covering the identity surfaces that exist solely under tracker-less mode.

The group must ship with a named input that turns it red. A fixture nobody can make fail is not a test, and this repository has shipped that shape before.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-STE-450.1: Fixture group 9 exists in `CANONICAL_FIXTURE_GROUPS` with `legs: ["none"]`, and a corresponding `#### Fixture group 9` block exists in the smoke-test skill's runtime-check phase. {#AC-STE-450.1}
- AC-STE-450.2: The group asserts gate probe #13's enforcing arm under tracker-less mode — a locally minted `id:` is required and well-formed, and the `tracker:` block is forbidden. {#AC-STE-450.2}
- AC-STE-450.3: The group asserts gate probe #73's enforcing arm — the minted plan's recorded identity self-derives its own filename. {#AC-STE-450.3}
- AC-STE-450.4: The group asserts that gate probe #26's skip text routes through `renderProbeSkipReason` and does NOT contain the phrase `require Linear MCP`. {#AC-STE-450.4}
- AC-STE-450.5: A named, documented input turns fixture group 9 RED, and that input is recorded in this FR's implementation notes. The mutation is executed as part of the FR's verification, not merely described. {#AC-STE-450.5}

## Technical Design

Registering a ninth group moves the roster count, which is pinned in several test files. That is a known cost of adding a group in this repository and is mechanical rather than risky — the count assertions are the point of the roster.

The three assertions are independent and should be able to fail independently within the group, so a single failure names which identity surface broke rather than reporting the group as generically red.

AC.4 is the cheapest of the three and pins a real cross-mode leak: a skip reason authored for the tracker case can survive into a mode where the remedy it names is nonsense.

## Testing

The group's own falsifiability is AC.5. The candidate mutations are concrete: emit a tracker block alongside a minted identity and confirm the probe-13 assertion fails; write a plan whose recorded identity disagrees with its filename and confirm the probe-73 assertion fails; restore the pre-renderer skip text and confirm the leak check fails.

At least one of those must be executed and its red result recorded.

Full gate green from the plugin root.

## Notes

Depends on STE-449. The audit may adjust what this group needs to assert.

Kept deliberately separate from STE-451. Merging the identity and lock-lifecycle surfaces into a single tracker-less group would mean one red signal for two unrelated failures, and the point of a per-group roster is to name which surface broke.

Registering a new group is the largest change this phase of the smoke skill has taken in its history, together with STE-451. That is stated as a fact about the work, not as a reason to reduce it.

## Implementation notes

### The headline result — AC.4's premise is FALSE at runtime, and the AC ships Partial

AC-STE-450.4 asks the group to assert that probe #26's skip text "routes through `renderProbeSkipReason`". **It does not route through anything, because there is nothing to route.** Measured, not inferred:

- `runTrackerProjectMilestoneAttachedProbe` returns a bare `{ violations: [], advisories: [] }` on its `mode: none` path. It emits **no text at all** — the words "skip"/"skipped" appear in that module only in a header comment.
- `renderProbeSkipReason` has **zero production callers**. Every importer in the repository is a test or this FR's own test file. The "MUST route through" sentence lives in `skills/gate-check/SKILL.md` as a **directive to the model**, not as a wired call.

So AC.4 splits, and it is reported honestly rather than rounded up:

- **Done** — the forbidden-phrase half. Enforced over *every* cause the renderer supports, executed rather than read, and paired with a positive assertion (`mcp_unavailable` legitimately names MCP; `mode_none` must not borrow its prose) so "the bad string is gone" cannot be satisfied by a renderer that returns nothing.
- **Partial** — the routing half. Enforced as far as it can be without wiring the probe: a **derived** drift pin binding the gate-check directive to the module's own exported `FORBIDDEN_SKIP_PHRASE`, **scoped to the directive's own line**. Sub-fixture 9c states the gap in its own prose so a green result cannot be read as evidence the routing exists.

Wiring the probe is a contract change to a shipped surface no AC of this FR authorizes, and `specs/plan/M121.md`'s blast-radius table lists the renderer as *asserted against, modified only if the leak check fails* — it did not fail. Recorded at `specs/notes/follow-ups.md` § 0g with a proposed scope, not absorbed.

### The three assertions fail INDEPENDENTLY — measured as a matrix, not asserted as a design intention

| input | 9a (probe #13) | 9b (probe #73) | 9c (skip leak) |
|---|---|---|---|

### The roster's `legs` field is now demonstrably N-way

Before this FR every roster was either the whole registered leg set or a subset of `{linear, jira}` — a shape a two-state flag models exactly. Group 2 alone could not settle it: one exemption on the leg the field was written around is what a flag looks like too. Group 9 is the first roster a flag cannot express, and it is a **third exemption reason class** pointing the other way — groups 2 and 4 are exempt *from* the tracker-less leg, group 9 is exempt from the *tracker* legs, because probes #13 and #73 do not go vacuous under a tracker, they **invert**. That distinction is written into `DECLARED_EXEMPTIONS`, the one site that structurally forces a new exemption to state its reason.

Measured consequence for the driver, recorded because it looks like a regression and is not: a tracker-less run that renders the block without group 9 now reports `STE-321 runtime check: NOT-REACHED`, `Fixture groups: FAIL`, rc 1. That is the correct answer — the leg genuinely gained a group it must run — not a fixture that needs relaxing.

### Correction tally, carried forward — the milestone is now at TWENTY-NINE

STE-449 left it at twenty. This FR produced **nine**: two found by re-reading my own additions, six by an adversarial pass run after I had already executed eleven mutations and called it done, and **one more by the end-of-FR AUDIT run after I had fixed all eight of those.** **That is the fourth consecutive FR in which a post-completion stage found real vacuity, and the third in which the finding was inside the implementer's own new test.** The gate was green — at 6814/0, then 6815/0 — for every one of them.

**The shape of that sequence is the finding.** Three independent verification stages ran here, and each one found something the previous had not: my own mutation set (eleven, all RED) missed the vacuity entirely because a mutation set only probes assertions you already believe in; the adversarial pass caught six by asking what would happen if I were *wrong*; and the audit caught a ninth after the adversarial pass had declared the file's 28 tests all able to fail — its verdict was right about the tests and wrong about one assertion inside one of them. **Diminishing, but never zero.**

21. **I wrote an assertion whose expected value was a computation, not a value.** The skip-text matrix row read `expect(await evaluate(f)).toEqual({ a: true, b: false === false, c: false })`. `false === false` is `true`, which happened to be the correct expectation, so the test passed and nothing would ever have flagged it. Had the correct expectation been `false`, that expression would have silently asserted the opposite — in the very test whose job is to prove independence. Harmless in outcome, and that is why it is worth recording: a green suite is not evidence that an assertion says what its author meant.

22. **I ran a mutation, watched it go RED, and drew a conclusion it did not support — then wrote that conclusion into this FR.** My gate-check directive pin was document-wide (`expect(gateDoc).toContain("renderProbeSkipReason")`), which is a genuinely weaker shape, so I narrowed it to the directive's own line and ran mutation 8 (delete the directive) to prove the narrowing bites. It went red, and I recorded that as proof. **It was not proof.** MEASURED afterwards: each token occurs *exactly once* in that SKILL, so deleting the directive reddens the document-wide pin too — mutation 8 cannot distinguish the two pins and my justifying comment ("both tokens occur elsewhere in this SKILL") was simply false. The distinguishing case is mutation 11: delete the directive while any one token survives anywhere else, where a document-wide `toContain` measurably returns `true` and the scoped pin goes RED. **A mutation only proves what it can distinguish, and a RED under the new form says nothing until you know the old form stayed green.** Pattern 31's own rider says narrowing is the same edit as blinding and only the mutation tells them apart — it needs a second rider: run the mutation against the form you are replacing, not only the one you are shipping.

23. **I broke a TRUE claim while fixing a false one.** The SKILL said *"Groups 4–8 spell out only their `PASS` and `FAIL` branches"* — accurate. Sweeping stale group-count arithmetic, I generalized it to *"Groups 4 and up"* — and group 9's own footer names all four outcomes, so the generalization was false the moment I wrote it. This is correction #19's shape **inverted**: that one was a fix whose scope stopped short of the defect; this one is a fix whose scope overshot into text that was already correct. Both read as finished. Restored to the bounded form.

24. **I introduced a new unpinned numeric claim in the same edit that removed unpinned numeric claims.** Replacing *"each of the eight fixture groups"* I wrote *"the roster is the authority and it has grown twice"* — a sentence that tells the reader not to trust counts in prose and then states one. It is false under both readings (the roster module was created carrying eight groups, so it has grown *once*; the SKILL's blocks grew *three* times) and nothing pins it. Deleted rather than corrected: the sentence's job was to say "read the roster", and any number in it undoes that.

25. **My own central non-vacuity guard compared a string with itself.** `evaluate()`'s 9c predicate asserted `capture.includes(canonicalSkipLine())` — but the synthetic capture was *built by interpolating* `canonicalSkipLine()`. True for any renderer output including the empty string. Its comment claimed it existed "because a ban alone is satisfied by a capture that says nothing at all", which is exactly the case it could not detect: measured, making the renderer return `""` left every row of the independence matrix green. Same shape as STE-449's #17 and #20, in the FR whose thesis is that such guards are the defect. Repaired by hand-restating the canonical line as a constant and adding mutation 9's drift pin so the restatement cannot silently diverge from its source.

26. **My stale-prose sweep stopped at the source module and left five mirrors.** I corrected *"the eight canonical records still decide it"* in `smoke_fixture_groups.ts` and left the byte-identical sentence in the test that mirrors it, plus two more "returns eight records" comments in the same file, plus — worse — the comment block *directly above the exemption map I had just given a third entry* still reading *"Two exemptions"*, *"the other seven"*, *"the one documented by-design exemption"*. **This is the third consecutive appearance of correction #19's class in this milestone, and the second time the same author reproduced it immediately after documenting it.** The general lesson stands and is evidently not enough on its own: when a defect class is "X went stale after Y widened", enumerate every place the statement could live *before* fixing the one in front of you — and grep for the sentence, not for the concept.

27. **A pin satisfied by the template describing the failure it no longer detects.** `expect(slice).toContain("GATE FAILED")` was meant to pin the group's RED-producing assertions. `GATE FAILED` occurs four times in the block and two are inside the `<…>` placeholders of the failure-diagnostic templates — so deleting the phrase from 9a's *operative* instruction produced **zero** failures (measured). Narrowed to the two `- **Violation.**` bullets, which mutation 10 confirms now bites.

28. **A scripted line replacement silently ate a clause.** Repairing the stale mirrors with a `python` line-index edit, I replaced a full comment line with a shorter one and dropped `construction. So a` from the middle of a sentence. Nothing catches this — comments do not compile-fail and no test reads them — and it was found only by reading the result. Line-index rewriting is convenient for numeric pins and hostile to prose; for prose, match the sentence.

29. **A THIRD positive pin that could not fail — found by the end-of-FR AUDIT, after eleven mutations and a full adversarial pass had both cleared the file.** `expect(report.severity).toBe("error")`, asserted on probe #13's report after staging the tracker-block violation. `runIdentityModeConditionalProbe` returns `severity: IDENTITY_MODE_CONDITIONAL_SEVERITY`, a module constant, **never derived from the violations**. MEASURED: a clean `mode: none` tree with **zero** violations also reports `severity: "error"`. The line read as evidence about the staged violation and was evidence about a constant. **This is correction #27's class committed a third time in one FR, by an author who had just written the Pattern 31 rider describing it** — which is the strongest argument available that the rider needed writing, and the strongest argument that writing it is not sufficient. Replaced with the row's `line`, recomputed from the mutated file, so a probe reporting the right violation at the wrong location now fails (mutation 12 confirms; the constant could not).

**The Pattern 31 collision did not fire this time, and it was designed around rather than dodged.** This FR's prose quotes the forbidden phrase in order to explain the ban, so a whole-file `not.toContain` on the smoke driver would have fired on the paragraph documenting it — the eleventh instance. Every ban here is scoped to the artifact that would actually carry a regression (a rendered string, a synthetic capture), never to the documents that discuss it. **But note what corrections #22 and #27 add to that pattern: the collision is only its negative half.** Pattern 31 is written about bans firing where they should not; both of this FR's pin defects were *positive* pins succeeding where they should not — the same wrong-scope root cause, the opposite symptom, and far harder to notice, because a spurious green raises no alarm at all.

### A process hazard worth naming — a delegated measurement mutated the working tree

The blast-radius survey was measured empirically by a subagent that registered a synthetic ninth group, ran the suite, and reverted. The method was sound and the result was excellent — it caught two sites I would have missed, including the `not-reached` count that must **stay** 8 while the record count moves to 9. But it left the tree mutated mid-run, and I noticed only because an unrelated `Edit` failed with *"file has been modified since read"*. **Delegating a mutation probe delegates the restore obligation with it, and the delegator cannot see the mutation window.** Verify the tree with `git status` before and after any delegated measurement, and never apply an edit to a file a subagent may be holding. Added to follow-ups § 0b.
