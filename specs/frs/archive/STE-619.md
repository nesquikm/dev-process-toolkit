# The review round records the direction four shipped ACs promised for it

## Summary

Four acceptance criteria of this milestone promised that every clause asserting new behaviour fails on the pre-change bytes and passes on the post-change bytes, with the direction recorded per clause.
Those criteria were closed when the milestone was archived, and the review round that followed added about 1,650 lines of new clauses without recording a direction for any of them.
The milestone's own later commits made four of its shipped criteria false; this record repairs them.

## Requirement

AC-STE-601.17, AC-STE-613.9, AC-STE-614.18 and AC-STE-615.8 each carry the same promise, and each was closed at the archive commit `c7ea732`. The post-release review round then landed `e4f8464`, `b1d5052` and `a167099`, adding four suites and roughly 1,650 lines of clauses. None of the four archived records covers them, and a search of `specs/` for the fix commits or the new suite names returns nothing.

The archive is not rewritten to make a stale record true. Amending four frozen files so an old promise reads as kept is the document-level form of re-measuring to pass a gate, and it would cost the archive the property that makes it worth having. The clauses are not those FRs' work either — they arrived after those FRs closed, and belong to the review round. So the direction record belongs to a record of its own, and the original criteria are read as scoped to the clauses their own FR shipped.

**This record is legitimate only as a transient.** Measured on a synthetic tree: probe #27 reports one violation for an ACTIVE feature request bound to an archived milestone (`active FR pointing at archived M_85e846`) and zero for an archived one. So this file reds the gate while it is open and clears the moment it is archived, which is why it is opened and archived inside one round. That is the second red this ceremony creates by design — probe #63's archive-then-ship gap is the first — and two of them is a pattern worth naming: a ceremony that teaches operators to expect red is how a real red gets waved through.

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-STE-619.1: Every clause added by the review round carries a recorded direction — falsifying, exempt by construction with its category named, or a non-discriminator named individually.
- AC-STE-619.2: The four criteria this record repairs are named, and the reason they became false is stated plainly rather than presented as routine new work.
- AC-STE-619.3: The measurement is taken per suite against the commit immediately before the one that introduced it, with every source file left at base bytes, and the run reports any suite that could not be measured rather than counting it green.

## Technical Design

Four throwaway worktrees under the temporary directory, each detached at its own base, each carrying only the suite file and the one test helper the review round changed. Every source file stays at base bytes. Each suite runs alone in both trees with the junit reporter, joined on nested describe path plus test name.

## Testing

The measurement is the test. Case counts must match between trees, and a base failure must be a real assertion failure rather than a module or fixture error — the leg-1 pathology recorded in the archived STE-614 record.

## Falsifiability

### The five non-discriminators, named

Each is a weakness in the clause, not in the code. All five remain real forward guards; none records a direction.

1. Suite A — "`simpleCommandWords` is declared in exactly one shipped module." Vacuously true at `c71a8e9`, where the front door tokenised nothing and so had no copy to be a second one. It carries the "rather than a second copy of the grammar" half of the fix's promise and measures none of it.
2. Suite C — "CLAIMED names the checkout that took the window, and does not claim the receipt predates anything." Already true at `e4f8464`; its own sibling row is marked green-today and this twin is not. Mislabelled, not broken.
3. Suite C — "OUT OF WINDOW says the receipt fell outside every window, names no other checkout, and claims no other run recorded it." Same shape as item 2.
4. Suite D — "every refusal names the checkout it is about, and none names the other one." Self-described in source as a negative control on the other axis; the base already satisfied it across the states it had.
5. Suite D — "the remedy never hands over the minting front door, in any sub-state." An invariant pin over new prose; the base prose never named the front door either.

### Finding: one suite's own direction claim is false for five rows

`tests/m_85e846-review-vouch-cause-diagnostics.test.ts` states in its header that every row not marked green-today is red at `e4f8464`. Measured, five unmarked rows are green there: the two named above, plus "every one of the three still REFUSES — the split does not open a hole", "`not-vouched` keeps naming the CLAIMED state", and the bare-absolute permit row. The last three are preservation or permit twins and are exempt by kind; they are still not what the header says they are. The other three suites make no blanket direction claim, and their in-source group comments match the measurement exactly.

This is the same defect the milestone is about, in the instrument rather than the subject: a claim in text that misdescribes what was measured.

## What ships as a known defect

Four defects in the refusal surface ship unfixed, by a stopping rule set before the round that found them: three consecutive rounds of fixes had each introduced a new false-cause state, so a fourth recurrence was treated as evidence about the method rather than grounds for another round. They are recorded here in full rather than softened.

**None of these is a security regression.** The leak this milestone exists to close stays closed, and every state below REFUSES correctly. What is wrong is what the operator is *told*. That distinction is the entire reason shipping is defensible: a gate that refuses the right action while misdescribing why is a documentation defect, not a hole.

1. **The order claim survives in its mirror half.** An announcement landing before the first window but after a retired or untimed call still takes the false "written before the first Skill call" sentence. Reachable by an ordinary path: interrupt a `/gate-check`, mint by hand, run `/gate-check` again.
2. **`between-windows` says "before the next one" where there is no next one.** Introduced by the commit that fixed the first half of defect 1. Here the NAME is false of the state, not only the sentence.
3. **A detected tamper is still dropped on most refusal branches.** `evidence.tampered` is carried out of every branch and read by nobody unless the reason is `announcement-tampered`, so a forgery detected alongside a different refusal is discarded silently. The permit branch reports it; the refusing branches do not. The comment justifying that is itself false of the states that produce it.
4. **`no-window` is untouched**, deferred deliberately — see below.

### Why `no-window` was deferred, and the measurement behind it

`no-window` fires only when a Skill call carries no parseable timestamp. Measured over the real corpus: **zero of 4,303 transcript records lack a timestamp**, so the state is near-unreachable outside a forged transcript. It is a known-false clause in a state an operator is not expected to reach.

This deferral previously lived only in a test-file comment. A decision recorded where nobody looks is not a decision anyone can find, so it is recorded here, with its measurement, where the record of this milestone is. The test-file comment stays where it is, deliberately: it tells a reader of that suite why a state is ungraded, in the place they would ask. Duplication was never the problem — a decision reachable only from inside the instrument was.

### The pattern underneath all four

Defect 2 was introduced by the fix for defect 1, and defect 1 was only half fixed: the named case was closed and its symmetric twin left open. **That is the third time in this milestone that a fix reached the named case and not its mirror** — the first being an audit report whose cited ordering was the one that passed while the opposite ordering leaked, the second a suite that graded one sub-state of a condition covering several. The lesson recurred inside the fix written after it was learned.

These states are composites, and patching them one sentence at a time cannot make them honest. That belongs to the redesign milestone, which now carries four structural findings: the absent typecheck gate, the two reds this ceremony creates by design, the collision between probe #27 and archive immutability, and this state model.

## Amending an archived record — the boundary

This file was amended after it was archived, to add the method caveat above. That is permitted, and the permission is narrow. An archived feature request may be amended only when ALL of these hold: it was authored in the round doing the amending; it has not shipped; the amendment adds method or context; it changes no claim, no number and no acceptance-criterion outcome; and the amendment is disclosed in the stage fence. Fail any one of the five and it is a revert, not a judgement call.

The rule this narrows is a behavioural constraint on what a skill writes at move time (`skills/spec-archive/SKILL.md`, codified as AC-STE-18.4), not an invariant the tree enforces — no probe grades post-archive edits as tree state, and the repository already carries a sanctioned exception in the same area, where traceability-link rewriting writes into archived plans. The purpose of the rule is that frozen history cannot be rewritten to make a stale promise read as kept. Adding a reproduction caveat to a record written in the same round, never shipped, changing no claim, does not engage that purpose. Fragmenting it would be worse: a caveat about how to reproduce a record belongs in the record, because the record's value is being findable.

## Two of the ceremony's own rules collide here

Probe #27 forces a feature request bound to an archived milestone to be archived before the round that authored it has finished — that is the transient recorded above. Archive immutability then forbids amending it. **Those two rules cannot both be satisfied by a record that is written and archived inside one round**, and this record walked into that contradiction by construction rather than by anyone's mistake.

It is the third structural finding of this kind, alongside the absent typecheck gate and the two reds the ceremony creates by design. All three are candidates for a redesign milestone, and all three share a property worth stating plainly: each was found by a human-scale accident — a commit refused, a record amended, a count that would not reconcile — and not by any gate. A ceremony whose rules disagree at the edges will keep producing these, and the edges are exactly where nobody is looking.
